001 The brief

An agency that sells AI had to look like it.

Cosmetics Growth helps high-end cosmetic dental practices win more patients through paid ads and AI automation. The founder came from a software background and wanted a site that felt genuinely engineered, not another templated agency page. It had to read premium and technical, build enough trust to justify a pay-per-appointment model, and turn cold visitors into booked strategy calls.

AUDIENCE
Elite practice owners

Busy, skeptical cosmetic dentists who've been burned by generalist agencies before.

GOAL
Booked strategy calls

Every section bends toward one action: book a call. The whole page is a single funnel.

TONE
Engineered & premium

Dark, precise, system-like, closer to a tech product than a dental brochure.

002 The real challenge

What the client needs beats what I think looks nice.

The hardest part wasn't making it look good, it was making it work. A beautiful site that doesn't book calls is a failed site. So every decision got measured against one question: does this make a skeptical dentist more likely to act? That turned the whole project from visual design into conversion design and front-end engineering.

“Looks nice” would have been…
  • Stock smiles and soft gradients
  • Decoration that slows the page down
  • Clever layouts that bury the offer
“Works” meant…
  • A clear promise above the fold
  • Proof and risk-reversal at every step
  • Fast, smooth, never in the way of the CTA
003 Iterating with the client

My first draft was pretty. It wasn't right yet.

My first version leaned on stock clinical photos and a polished but generic agency layout, the kind of thing I thought looked good. Then I sat down with the founder. He told me what dentists actually respond to, and we rebuilt it into something sharper, more technical, and led by the offer instead of the imagery.

005 Light & dark rhythm

Dark cards that float on calm light.

The page breathes between airy light sections and deep, glowing dark cards. Those dark moments are where the asks live, the offer, the closing CTA, so the eye is pulled straight to them. Cyan does exactly one job: mark the single most important word in view.

Bright cyan flags the key action
Near-black cards carry the conversions

007 Designing around the offer

The offer was the hardest thing to make believable.

“Only pay when a patient shows up” sounds too good, so the design had to defuse the skepticism, not amplify it. Plain language, visible pricing, and an explicit “no-shows are not billable” line do more for conversion than any flourish could.

This is where the founder's feedback mattered most: he knew exactly what dentists push back on, and I'd redesign the section until the objection disappeared.

Reflection & next steps

What this first client taught me.

My instinct was to make something that looked impressive. The founder kept pulling me back to what the business actually needed, and the version we landed on is sharper for it. Designing with a client beats designing at them.

Building it myself meant I owned the trade-offs end to end: if an effect hurt load time or distracted from the CTA, it didn't ship. Effectiveness became the brief, and prettiness had to earn its place inside it.

Next: test the hero headline
A/B the promise-led headline against a proof-led one to see which books more calls.
Next: build out the funnel pages
Procedure-specific landing pages (veneers, All-on-4, implants) on the same system.
Next: instrument & measure
Add scroll-depth and CTA analytics so future changes are decided by data, not taste.
