001 The thesis

A designer's site should be a demo, not a CV.

Anyone can write “I build interactive front-ends.” I wanted you to feel it in the first five seconds. So I gave myself one rule: every claim on the page needs an interaction that proves it. Keep it clean and fast. Keep it mine.

Show the craft

Prove the front-end skills live, in the page itself, not in a bullet list.

Have a personality

The cat mascot and playful touches make it memorable, not just another grid.

Stay out of the way

Effects add delight without slowing the page or burying the work.

Reflection

What my first portfolio taught me.

Building the interactions myself meant I couldn't fake the skill, and that was the point. Coding the eye-tracking, the doodle saving, and the scroll highlight turned “I do front-end” into something a visitor experiences instead of reads.

It also taught me restraint. The first draft had effects everywhere; the final one keeps the palette quiet and the type loud, so the personality lands without the page feeling busy.
